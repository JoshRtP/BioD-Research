# Provisional final recommendation: Brazilian biodiversity measurement architecture

## Executive recommendation

Adopt a nested, auditable architecture rather than one biodiversity score. Begin with six non-negotiable safeguards, require a small universal set of pressure and condition metrics, trigger landscape/field metrics only where material, and reserve biological-response evidence for leadership/pilot claims. Failed safeguards must never be offset.

## Program architecture

| Program level | Requirements | Monitoring | Assurance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entry/eligibility | M01–M06 safeguards | Continuous records; annual and event screens | Independent risk-based verification; material failure blocks eligibility |
| Core improvement | M07–M11 and universal M12 screen | Annual pressure/management; condition 2–3 yearly | Records/GIS reconciliation plus field sample |
| Conditional core | M09/M13–M15/M18 when triggers apply | Protocol-specific; landscape coordination where needed | Technical review and risk-based field verification |
| Leadership/outcome | M16 and validated outcome methods | Repeated fit-for-purpose surveys | Independent technical verification; uncertainty/attribution disclosed |
| Research | M17 and unvalidated tools | Pilot only | No public outcome claim |

## Baseline, target, remediation, and escalation rules

1. Conversion uses a fixed historical cutoff; all other metrics use the first complete verified baseline unless a justified reference condition is approved.
2. Set numeric trajectories only after the pilot establishes feasibility, variance, and minimum detectable change.
3. Safeguard failure triggers immediate containment, investigation, transparent disposition, remediation where possible, and eligibility/claims suspension as appropriate.
4. Missing safeguard evidence is a failed eligibility check until resolved. Missing improvement data receives no positive score and must be reported.
5. Record reversals; restoration or purchased credits cannot cure prohibited conversion or rights violations.
6. Require root-cause and corrective-action plans for stalled/deteriorating core metrics; escalate repeated or systemic issues to procurement controls.

## Reporting template

Every annual report should disclose:

- total and traceable volume/property coverage; direct versus indirect sourcing;
- count/area/volume of safeguard failures, unknowns, suspensions, and remediation status;
- baseline, current value, change, denominator, uncertainty, and missingness for each metric;
- results by biome/supply shed and producer segment where privacy permits;
- changes in methods, sources, boundaries, or suppliers;
- stakeholder grievances and resolutions without exposing sensitive data;
- assurance scope, sampling, findings, and limitations;
- claim wording linked to the measured evidence layer.

## Claims and assurance rules

- Never use “biodiversity positive,” “nature positive,” “restored,” or equivalent broad claims from practice adoption or a composite score.
- State cutoff, ecosystem scope, sourcing coverage, spatial boundary, reporting period, and assurance for no-conversion claims.
- State whether change is observed, modeled, or inferred; disclose uncertainty and attribution limits.
- Keep credits/finance claims separate from sourcing claims and require an independently validated method for additionality, leakage, permanence/reversal, rights, and benefit sharing.
- Assurance providers must be independent from metric sales/implementation conflicts and use a transparent risk-based sampling method.

## Phased adoption roadmap

| Timing after approval | Action | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 months | Approve scope; appoint governance bodies; legal/rights review; obtain current source annexes and Brazil interpretations | Signed scope, claims register, source register, legal issues log |
| 3–6 months | Validate crosswalk; stakeholder scoring; configure data governance and grievance; recruit pilot | Documented decisions and representative pilot sample |
| 6–18 months | Run pilot baseline/repeatability; test cost, data pathways, assurance, incentives | Technical and rights panels approve/reject each metric |
| 18–24 months | Set numeric trajectories; publish program manual/claims policy; train and phase suppliers | Versioned requirements and assurance readiness |
| Year 3+ | Scale core set; add validated conditional/leadership metrics; evaluate outcomes and equity | Independent evaluation and improvement cycle |

## Priority research agenda

1. State-level legal requirements and enforcement-data interpretation in target sourcing states.
2. Biome-specific reference condition, restoration milestones, and condition-index validation.
3. Transparent pesticide toxic-load method calibrated to Brazilian crops/ecosystems.
4. Connectivity and leakage methods that fairly allocate landscape accountability.
5. Biological-response survey power, detection, seasonality, and costs.
6. Burden and exclusion effects for smaller/indirect suppliers; effectiveness of group support.
7. Data sovereignty, consent, benefit sharing, and grievance effectiveness.
8. Current versions of SBTN methods, SAI supporting documents, RTRS/Brazil interpretations, and other selected standards.

## Limitations and correct use

This is a complete **desk-research execution package**, not a completed participatory or empirical research project. The recommendation is provisional until Brazilian legal/rightsholder review, stakeholder deliberation, and pilot evidence are complete. The files explicitly identify the remaining decisions and provide the instruments needed to complete them without overstating evidence.
