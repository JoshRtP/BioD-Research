## Key document-confirmed findings used in the crosswalk

- RTRS V4.0 applies to all scales of soy production and uses the whole farm as the certification unit; it expects baselines and monitoring where indicators require monitoring (p. 4).
- RTRS 4.4.2 prohibits conversion in natural land after 3 June 2016; 4.5 requires mapping, a monitored native-vegetation/wildlife plan, threatened-species protection, and conservation action for farms below 10% native vegetation (pp. 25–26).
- RTRS Annex 5 explicitly offers habitat extent, canopy cover, distance/connectivity, bare soil, species/plant measures, water indicators, fire, and spray-drift evidence as example indicators (pp. 47–50).
- ProTerra MRV V1.0 verifies economic-operator management systems through independent third-party audit and applies to agricultural-commodity buyers/traders/storage operators (pp. 4–5).
- ProTerra supplier requirements include riparian/floodplain maintenance or restoration, wetland protection, threatened-species protection, water-quality protection, water-use monitoring, soil practices, and erosion prevention (pp. 14–16).

## Crosswalk consensus and differentiation

### Strong consensus

- Legal compliance, traceability, no conversion/deforestation, protection of native vegetation/riparian areas/wetlands and threatened species, responsible agrochemical management, soil/water protection, monitoring, remediation, and rights safeguards.

### Differentiation

- **RTRS:** strongest supplied farm/soy-specific biodiversity plan and farm-practice detail.
- **ProTerra MRV:** strongest supplied economic-operator due-diligence, supplier-risk, procurement-control, and chain monitoring frame.
- **SBTN:** strongest supplied prioritization/target architecture.
- **SAI:** strongest supplied regenerative-transition and implementation-group framing.
- **IETA:** claims/market warning layer, not a sourcing standard.

### Blind spots requiring added methods or validation

1. Biome-specific reference condition and numeric condition thresholds.
2. Comparable pesticide toxic-load protocol and thresholds.
3. Statistically defensible biological-response protocols.
4. Landscape leakage and connectivity accountability.
5. Cost, burden, accuracy, and repeatability evidence by producer segment.
6. Full current state-level Brazilian legal crosswalk.
7. Current SBTN target methods and current SAI supporting assurance/quantification documents.
8. Additionality, permanence, reversals, and benefit sharing for any credit claim.
