# Brazil context: legal screen, materiality, and spatial data

**Status:** desk-research decision aid, checked 12 June 2026. It is not a Brazilian legal opinion. State/municipal rules and case-specific licensing may be stricter. A qualified Brazilian legal and rights review is mandatory before implementation.

## 1. Federal legal/policy screen

| Topic | Authoritative instrument/source to verify | Program treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Native vegetation, APP, Legal Reserve, CAR/PRA | Law 12,651/2012 (Native Vegetation Protection Law/Forest Code), including amendments; official CAR/SICAR records | Required legal safeguard plus no-conversion rule; verify CAR status but do not treat registration alone as compliance |
| Environmental crimes and sanctions | Law 9,605/1998 and Decree 6,514/2008, as amended | Required legal safeguard; screen embargoes and unresolved material sanctions |
| Protected areas | Law 9,985/2000 (SNUC), management plans and buffer-zone rules | Required; exclude unauthorized activity and screen adjacency impacts |
| Indigenous and traditional-community rights | Constitution; applicable statutes; ILO Convention 169; official FUNAI and other competent-agency information | Rights safeguard, conflict screen, meaningful participation and FPIC where applicable; no certification score can compensate for failure |
| Water use and quality | Law 9,433/1997, CONAMA rules, basin/state permits | Legal compliance plus conditional water-risk and water-quality metrics |
| Pesticides | Law 14,785/2023 and current implementing/registration restrictions | Legal compliance plus voluntary toxic-load reduction; maintain current prohibited/restricted list |
| Threatened species | Current MMA/ICMBio/JBRJ official lists; note 2026 aquatic-fauna updates | Required protection; species metric conditional on likely presence and material risk |
| Biodiversity policy | National Biodiversity Strategy/action instruments and Kunming–Montreal GBF implementation | Alignment context, not farm certification equivalence |

### Legal-versus-voluntary decision rules

1. Apply the strictest applicable federal, state, municipal, permit, certification, and buyer rule.
2. Treat legality as the floor, never proof of a biodiversity outcome.
3. Use the full rural-property boundary for safeguard screening, including non-crop areas.
4. A CAR entry is a traceability/data source, not conclusive evidence of validated compliance.
5. Do not allow restoration elsewhere or purchased credits to cure post-cutoff conversion or rights violations.
6. Escalate land/tenure conflicts, overlapping claims, protected-area/territory overlap, and community allegations to qualified review before sourcing eligibility.

### Applicability implications

- **All biomes:** safeguards, habitat extent/loss, habitat plan/condition, toxic load, soil cover/erosion, riparian/wetland protection, and rights screen.
- **Cerrado/Atlantic Forest/Pampa:** prioritize connectivity because fragmentation and remaining habitat configuration matter.
- **Pantanal and connected headwaters:** hydrology, wetland integrity, fire, water quality, and landscape collaboration are essential.
- **Caatinga:** water stress, erosion, dryland reference condition, and locally appropriate restoration protocols require special treatment.
- **Amazon/Cerrado expansion frontiers:** indirect sourcing, leakage, fire, and conversion monitoring require portfolio/landscape controls.

## 3. Spatial and protocol catalogue

| Dataset/source | Primary use | Scale/frequency | Key limitation / control |
|---|---|---|---|
| IBGE biome and official territorial layers | Biome assignment and context | National; periodic | Too coarse for property condition; use current official version |
| SICAR/CAR and state validation systems | Property boundary and legal-declaration inputs | Property; status varies | Self-declared/validation status varies; resolve overlaps and do not infer compliance |
| INPE PRODES/DETER and relevant biome systems | Official deforestation monitoring and alerts | Annual/alerts; biome coverage differs | Alerts are not final determinations; combine with high-resolution imagery and field evidence |
| MapBiomas land cover/use and alerts | Historical land cover, conversion, water/fire context | Annual/alerts | Non-governmental; classification uncertainty; independently verify safeguard decisions |
| ANA/Hidroweb and basin/state water data | Water risk, flow, quality, basin context | Station/basin; variable | Sparse/uneven stations; farm impact attribution is difficult |
| ICMBio/MMA/JBRJ threatened-species lists and occurrence data | Species-risk screening | National lists; updates vary | Absence of a record is not absence; expert survey design needed |
| Protected areas, Indigenous lands and other official rights layers | Exclusion/rights/conflict screening | National; updates vary | Boundaries/status can change; confirm with competent authority and rights holders |
| Farm records and audit evidence | Inputs, practices, incidents, restoration | Field/property; annual/event | Gaming and completeness risks; reconcile purchases, application, and field checks |
| Field habitat plots / photo points | Condition and restoration verification | Stratified; 2–3 yearly | Protocol and reference ecosystem must be biome-specific |
| eDNA/acoustics/camera/pollinator surveys | Leadership biological response | Stratified; seasonal | Detection bias and expertise/cost; pilot before claims |

## 4. Producer burden and equity controls

- Keep safeguards identical, but permit group-managed mapping, training, records, and sampling for smaller producers.
- Provide no-cost/low-cost technical support and a remediation pathway for remediable data gaps; do not weaken rights or conversion rules.
- Separate inability to pay for sophisticated monitoring from poor ecological performance.
- Obtain consent for sensitive location/traditional-knowledge data; apply access controls and benefit-sharing rules.
